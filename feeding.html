Feeding & nutrition

Feed for a long, healthy life

Good nutrition is the single biggest lever you control for your pet's health. Learn how to choose the right food, portion it correctly, and avoid common mistakes.

Feeding checklistGet a feeding plan

Match food to life stage

Nutritional needs shift dramatically from youth to old age. Growing animals need more energy and specific nutrients, while seniors often need fewer calories and joint support. Always choose a complete and balanced food formulated for your pet's current life stage.

Get portions right

Overfeeding is one of the most common causes of pet health problems. Use the feeding guide on the packaging as a starting point, then adjust based on body condition and your vet's advice. Measure meals with a proper scoop rather than eyeballing them.

Tip: You should be able to feel your pet's ribs with light pressure and see a defined waist from above. If not, it may be time to adjust portions.

Read the label

Look for a named protein source, a statement of nutritional adequacy, and appropriate guidance for your species. Treats should make up no more than about ten percent of daily calories.

Foods to keep away from pets

  • Chocolate, caffeine, and alcohol
  • Grapes, raisins, onions, and garlic
  • Xylitol (a sweetener found in gum and some foods)
  • Avocado (especially dangerous for birds)
